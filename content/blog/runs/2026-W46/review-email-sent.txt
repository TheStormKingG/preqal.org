QUALITY MANAGEMENT

How long ISO 9001 certification takes in Guyana

Six to twelve months is what the national standards bureau tells companies to
expect. The audit at the end is the short part. What takes the time is building
enough working history for an auditor to have something to read.

-- Six to twelve months is the figure your own standards bureau uses

The standards body in Guyana puts a number on this. The Guyana National Bureau
of Standards says its implementation support usually runs six to twelve months
and follows a step-by-step approach. That spread is not padding. It depends on
how much of a working system you already have, and on whether the work keeps
moving after the first enthusiastic month.

[FIGURE fig-1.svg - "The build is the long bar. The audit is the thin one."]

The plan behind that number is not mysterious. The Bureau opens with a gap
analysis to work out what has to be built and what only needs changing. After
that come set periods for training on the requirements, for documentation, for
hands-on implementation and finally for a pre-assessment. Each block rests on
the one below it, which is why the order cannot be rearranged to save a month.

You will find pages online offering ISO 9001 in thirty days. What those pages
sell is a document pack with your company name typed into the header. A
certificate mill can write your quality manual in a fortnight, and it still
cannot make your management review happen, because that meeting has to be held
and minuted before anybody can audit it. The paperwork was never the slow part.

-- The build is long because the system has to run before it can be audited

Start with what is quick. Training courses at the Bureau are short, lasting
between one and four days. In a single year it ran fifty-seven courses and
trained about seven hundred people. So the teaching is not what holds anyone up.

What holds you up is evidence. An internal audit needs something to audit,
which means records that have piled up over months of ordinary work. A
management review needs a quarter of figures in front of it before the meeting
is worth holding. Those two records are the ones an auditor opens early, and
neither of them can be manufactured in the week before the visit.

Think of it as a driving test. The test itself takes about an hour. Nobody
passes it by reading the highway code on the morning, because what is being
measured is the hours already logged behind the wheel. Your quality system is
sat the same way, and the logbook is the part that takes the months.

-- A Guyanese company took about eighteen months from first help to certificate

There is a local case with dates on it. The Bureau worked with the team at
Metro Office and Computer Supplies through 2021 on implementing the
requirements of a quality management system. The certificate was presented on
12 July 2022. Count that and you get about eighteen months from the start of
real help to paper in hand.

The wider picture is slower, and it is the more honest one. Metro came up
through a regional trade project launched in 2019, under which six private
sector organisations across the region committed to achieve certification.
Metro was the first of the six. That still took three years from the start of
the project.

So the honest spread for a first ISO 9001 certificate is roughly eighteen
months at the quick end and three to five years at the slow end. The project
that carried Metro ran on across sixteen states in the region and closed only
in 2024. That range is not set by the standard. It is set by whether somebody
senior owns the work, and by whether the records keep being made in the months
when nothing is being audited.

You could do this without us. Yes, and some companies do, and a few of them
pass first time. What a consultant mainly buys you is speed and a much lower
chance of spending a second year closing findings you could have closed in the
first.

-- The clock does not stop when the certificate arrives

An ISO 9001 certificate runs on a three-year cycle, with a surveillance audit
in the first year after it is issued. When the Saint Lucia Bureau of Standards
sat its own first-year surveillance audit, the visit took two days. Two days is
nothing. Staying ready for those two days, across the three years between
certificates, is the whole job.

A second clock is running too. The International Organization for
Standardization has published a new edition of ISO 9001, and organisations
certified to the 2015 edition have three years to move to it. The move is
described as five steps rather than one event. Start any new build against the
new edition.

One number says how thin the certified population here still is. The Bureau
reported a total of eighty-five certified stakeholders after its most recent
round. That is the whole certified market you would be standing in, across
every sector and every scheme the Bureau runs. In a tender, scarcity works for
you.

-- What to do in the first fortnight so the twelve months does not become three years

None of the early work needs a budget. It needs somebody senior to own the five
decisions below.

  1. Write down the scope in one paragraph, naming the sites and the products
     it covers, before anybody writes a procedure.
  2. Ask the Bureau for a gap analysis, and treat the output as the project
     plan rather than as a report.
  3. Put a date in the diary now for the first management review, far enough
     out that there are figures to review.
  4. Decide who will run the internal audit, and make sure it is not the
     person who wrote the procedures.
  5. Ask your certification body, in writing, which edition of ISO 9001 your
     audit will be run against.

There is a smaller rung first. The Bureau runs a national quality management
system certification for smaller companies, valid for two years and monitored
in between. It exists because ISO 9001 was written with more complex
organisations in mind and asks for extensive documentation, formal review
systems and real resources. For a firm of twelve people that rung is usually
the sensible first move.

A quality system is not a document you buy. It is a crop. You put the work in
during months when nothing visible is happening, and the certificate is simply
the week somebody comes to weigh what grew.

-- Questions

Q: How long does ISO 9001 certification take in Guyana?
A: Six to twelve months is the published figure. The Guyana National Bureau of
Standards gives that range for its implementation support. A local company
certified in July 2022 took about eighteen months, and three to five years is
common where the work stalls between audits.

Q: Why does it take months rather than weeks?
A: Training is not the bottleneck. Courses run one to four days, but an
internal audit and a management review both need records built up over months.
The time goes on accumulating the working history an auditor can read.

Q: How long is the certificate valid, and what happens after?
A: The certification cycle runs three years, with a surveillance audit in the
first year after issue. One national bureau completed its first-year
surveillance audit in a two-day visit. Recertification comes at the end of the
three-year cycle and is a fuller audit than a surveillance visit.

Q: Is there a faster route for a small Guyanese business?
A: Yes. The Bureau runs a national quality management system certification
aimed at smaller companies, valid for two years and monitored in between. It is
lighter than ISO 9001, which was written with more complex organisations in
mind, and it works as a step towards the international standard.

-- Closing

The difference is made in the first fortnight. Naming an owner and putting the
first management review in the diary is what separates the six-to-twelve-month
version from the three-year one. Systems Builder(TM) sets that up and gets the
records being made. Certified Care(TM) keeps them coming in the quiet months,
which is what decides your first surveillance audit.
