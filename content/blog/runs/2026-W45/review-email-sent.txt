QUALITY MANAGEMENT
WHAT AN ISO 9001 AUDITOR OPENS FIRST

An audit of your quality system does not begin on the factory floor. It begins in a reading room, with a small set of records the auditor opens in a settled order. Four of them decide how the rest of the week goes.

## The audit starts in your documents, not on your floor

A first certification audit is run in two stages, and the first stage is mostly reading. The auditor wants to know whether there is a system there to audit at all. That stage has a name and a literature behind it. The ISO 9001 Auditing Practices Group publishes a paper on the two-stage initial certification audit, alongside papers on audit planning, on checklists and on evidence collection. None of those papers is about your floor.

[PHOTO wide] Two men sitting at a conference table, one speaking with an open notebook in front of him while the other listens.
    caption: The first stage of a certification audit is mostly reading. The floor comes later.
    credit: Unknown (see sourceUrl) / Unsplash / https://unsplash.com/photos/two-men-in-a-meeting-at-a-conference-table-3IJbPn9sL_w

That group has been running since 2003, and it is not a vendor. It is an informal group of quality management system experts, auditors and practitioners drawn from the International Organization for Standardization committee that owns ISO 9001 and from the accreditation side of the chain. Its papers are free to read. Read the one on evidence collection before your auditor does.

You will read online that the certification audit is the moment everything is decided. It is not. Most of what an auditor will conclude about your system is already sitting in four records, and those records were written months before anyone arrived at your gate.


## Four records tell the auditor whether the system is used

The four are your scope, your quality policy with its objectives, your last internal audit report and the minutes of your last management review. Take them in that order, because that is roughly the order they get opened in. The scope says what you claim to control. The policy and the objectives say what good looks like in numbers. The internal audit says whether you checked for yourself. The management review says whether anyone senior read the answer and did something about it.

The Auditing Practices Group arranges its own guidance along the same line, with separate papers on scope and applicability, on policy, objectives and management review, and on internal audit. The thinking in those papers follows the process-based approach and the risk-based thinking that auditing ISO 9001 requires. That is the whole test in one phrase. A system that exists only as a folder fails it.

The Guyana National Bureau of Standards lays the standard out the same way, naming Context of the Organisation, Leadership, Planning, Support, Operation and Performance Evaluation as the clauses that guide a working system. Performance Evaluation is the clause most systems are thinnest on. It is also the one the auditor reaches first.


## The standard your auditor works to was reissued this year

ISO 19011 is the guidance auditors themselves work from, and a new edition of it landed this year. It was released in May 2026 and announced by the quality management committee on 9 June 2026. The committee that owns ISO 9001 names it as the key standard to refer to for auditing against ISO 9001. So the rule book moved.

What changed is worth knowing before you sit across a table from an auditor. The 2026 edition is written to address the growing weight of technology, digitisation and virtual working, and it puts more emphasis on risk analysis and on mitigation. Remote and hybrid auditing is now ordinary rather than an exception. Expect more of your audit to happen through a screen.

There is a second standard behind the first, and it governs whether your auditor is competent to audit a quality system at all. It is ISO/IEC 17021-3, published in 2017, and it sets the competence requirements for bodies that audit and certify quality management systems. Nobody will volunteer this. Ask.

[WARNING] Ask your certification body which edition of ISO 19011 its auditors are working to, and get the answer in writing. A proposal written against the older edition is not wrong. It does tell you how current the body is, and that is worth knowing before you sign.


## In Guyana your first audit is usually not the certification audit

Most Guyanese businesses meet an auditor long before a certification body ever arrives. The Guyana National Bureau of Standards has been certified to ISO 9001 since 2018, and it runs training on the awareness and interpretation of the standard, on implementation, on internal auditing and on pre-assessment audits. It also runs an auditing programme of its own, giving an organisation an independent look at its own operations. That is a rehearsal, and a rehearsal is cheap.

The Bureau is not new to any of this. It was established under the Standards Act No. 11 of 1984, amended in 1997, and it opened in March 1985. It is governed by a National Standards Council whose members come from sectors across the economy. Four decades is a long memory.

[PHOTO inline] Four women in blue uniforms and hairnets sorting and rolling towels along a factory bench, with printed notices on the wall behind them.
    caption: The notices on that wall are part of the record. An auditor reads about them before walking past them.

You could skip the rehearsal and go straight at the certification audit. Yes, and some businesses do, and some of them pass. What a pre-assessment actually buys you is the list of findings while they are still cheap to fix, because a finding raised by the Bureau costs you a fortnight of work and the same finding raised at the real audit costs you a corrective action plan and a return visit.

There is also a rung below international certification, and it is often the right one to stand on first. The Bureau runs a national quality management system certification aimed at micro, small and medium enterprises in Guyana, built as a step towards the international standard. For a company of twelve people that is usually the sensible first move. It is a rung, not a detour.


## What to have ready, and in what order

None of this needs a project. It needs four folders and an afternoon to put them in order.

  - Write your scope in one paragraph, then check that it matches what you actually do and the sites you actually do it at.
  - Put your quality policy and this year's objectives on a single page, with the current figure written beside each objective.
  - Produce your last internal audit report, including the findings you have not closed yet. Especially those.
  - Produce the minutes of your last management review, signed, with a named owner against every action.
  - Ask your certification body, in writing, which edition of ISO 19011 and which edition of ISO 9001 your audit will be run against.

The thing to avoid is the folder written for the auditor. It reads beautifully and nobody in the building has opened it since the week it was printed. An auditor who has read four hundred of those can tell inside a page. Audits are there to assess conformity, to evaluate effectiveness and to find opportunities to improve, and effectiveness is exactly where the folder written for show falls apart.

It does work when it is done properly, and there is a Guyanese case to point at. Metro Office and Computer Supplies, a Guyana-located company, was certified to ISO 9001 in July 2022 under a regional trade programme. The Bureau worked with the Metro team through 2021 on implementing the requirements, and it was credited with an integral part in the result. That was about a year of work before anybody was audited.

"The auditor is not looking for your manual. The auditor is looking for the last four decisions your management actually made."  - Dr. Stefan Gravesande

Think of those four records as the house papers for your quality system. A buyer does not walk the rooms first. A buyer asks who owns the place, what the title covers and whether the last inspection turned anything up. Your auditor is a buyer.


## FAQs

Q. What does an ISO 9001 auditor look at first?
A. Your scope, your quality policy with its objectives, your last internal audit report and the minutes of your last management review. Those four say whether the system is used or only written. The floor and the interviews come after them.

Q. Is the first certification audit one visit or two?
A. Two stages. The first is largely a review of your documents and your readiness, which is why the ISO 9001 Auditing Practices Group publishes a paper on the two-stage initial certification audit. The second is the full audit against the standard.

Q. Which edition of ISO 19011 will my auditor use?
A. The current edition was released in May 2026 and announced on 9 June 2026, and it carries more emphasis on risk and on virtual working. Which edition your own auditor works to is a question for your certification body, and the answer belongs in writing.

Q. Can the Guyana National Bureau of Standards audit us before certification?
A. Yes. The Bureau offers pre-assessment audits and training on internal auditing, and it runs its own auditing programme. It also runs a national quality management system certification for micro, small and medium enterprises as a step towards the international standard.
