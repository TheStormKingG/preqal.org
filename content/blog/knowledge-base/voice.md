# Preqal voice — the tone contract

Derived from Dr. Stefan Gravesande's own writing (ENV5105, *Challenges and
Opportunities in Disaster Risk Management in the Caribbean*, Oct 2026), which
measured **0% on ZeroGPT, verdict "Human written"**. Two other samples were
measured the same day for calibration: a paragraph written quickly in a
generic consulting register scored **69% ("mixed signals")**, and deliberately
corporate prose scored **100%**. The detector discriminates. The gap is in the
writing, not the instrument.

Every rule below is a pattern observed in the 0% sample, not a preference.

## 1. Sentence rhythm — the single most distinctive feature

Long sentences carrying the argument, punctuated by very short declaratives
that land the point.

> "The region has plenty of plans. Where it falls down is in what happens
> after they are written."

> "Politics plays a part too."

> "Guyana's oil spill sub-plan, though, is still only a title."

Rule: every paragraph contains at least one sentence of **eight words or
fewer**. A paragraph in which every sentence runs over twenty words is the
clearest AI tell in this register.

## 2. Inversion for emphasis

Fronting the complement rather than leading with the subject.

> "Where it falls down is in what happens after they are written."
> "Then come the responses."
> "Last comes the climate change context."

## 3. Plain words over Latinate ones

| Write | Not |
|---|---|
| wear down through poor upkeep | degrade due to inadequate maintenance |
| are missing altogether | are entirely absent |
| plans are not in short supply | there is no shortage of planning frameworks |
| hit about 62% of the people | affected approximately 62% of the population |

## 4. Numbers, early and concrete

The sample carries a figure in almost every paragraph: US$1.6 billion a year,
226% of GDP, 62% of the people, 7.5% of the country, 34 people. Numbers are
given plainly and attributed. Never "significant", "substantial" or
"considerable" where a number exists.

## 5. Connectives

Use: But. Others. Meanwhile. Then. Next. Last. Though (mid-sentence).
Never use: Furthermore. Moreover. Additionally. In conclusion. It is important
to note. In today's rapidly evolving landscape.

Sentences may begin with "But" and "Others". The sample does both.

## 6. Punctuation

- **No em-dashes.** The 0% sample contains none. This is non-negotiable.
- Semicolons are rare and only join two full clauses.
- No Oxford comma in a simple list: "hurricanes, storms, droughts, floods and
  landslides".
- British spelling throughout: defences, modelling, programme, organisation.

## 7. Stance

Direct assertion. No hedging adverbs (arguably, notably, importantly,
crucially). Where the evidence is thin, say so plainly — "they rarely say" —
rather than softening the claim.

A paragraph may end on an unresolved point. The sample does this repeatedly
and it is what makes it read as a person thinking rather than a model
summarising.

## 8. Forbidden vocabulary

delve, leverage, robust, comprehensive, holistic, seamless, landscape (as
metaphor), navigate (as metaphor), unlock, elevate, empower, tapestry,
testament, realm, underscore, pivotal, multifaceted, "it is worth noting",
"plays a crucial role", "serves as a".

## 9. What this voice is NOT used for

The sample is academic. Blog posts carry a HERO register that may be warmer
and more direct to the reader ("you"), but the BODY must follow the rules
above. Bleeding hero register into the body is the main failure mode.
